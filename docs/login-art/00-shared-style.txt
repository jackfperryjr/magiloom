SHARED BLOCK — appended to every scene prompt
=============================================
(Reference copy. Each scene .txt already ends with this; edit here first, then
re-paste into the scene files so they stay identical.)

FORMAT
16:9 landscape, 2560x1440, full-bleed. A pure background painting for a fantasy
game's sign-in screen: nothing but the scene itself, edge to edge, with no
graphic or interface elements of any kind in the image.

COMPOSITION
Everything that holds the eye — the subject, the brightest values, the sharpest
detail — belongs in the middle and the right of the picture. The near-left is
the emptiest part of the scene: open ground running away with nothing in it
worth looking at. Leave the extreme bottom edge simple and unbusy.

The picture is one continuous scene, evenly lit and evenly painted from edge to
edge. Do not divide it into zones, and do not paint a band, column, ribbon,
panel, frame or box anywhere in it; do not darken, shade, tint, blur or
vignette one side relative to the other. The quiet side is quiet because
nothing is happening there, not because something has been laid over it.

LIGHT AND PALETTE
Low-key and dark overall. The brightest value in the frame is a small
highlight, not a broad area, and it sits with the subject. Accent hues, used
sparingly: periwinkle #6467dc and lantern amber #d07400.

STYLE
Painterly digital illustration with visible brushwork and soft cinematic
lighting. Part of a set with painted character portraits, so the same hand:
rich but restrained colour, forms read at a glance, no rendering fuss.

NEGATIVE
No text or lettering of any kind, no logo, no watermark, no signature, no
border or frame, no panel, no card, no ribbon, no banner, no box, no rectangle
of flat colour over any part of the image, no vignette, no darkened band or
shaded column down one side, no gradient overlay across the frame, no
photographic texture, no lens flare, no chromatic aberration, no HDR glow, no
vector-flat shapes, no people or faces unless the scene asks for them. No
labels, captions, names, numbers, arrows or leader lines anywhere in the
picture — this is a painting, not a diagram.

LOOMY — canonical description
=============================
(Used verbatim in the five scenes he appears in: downpour, harvest, hallows,
yule, fireworks. Pose, hat and lighting are per-scene; everything here is
invariant. His pose is the one thing worth varying hard — he is a ragdoll, so
he reads best mid-gesture: waving, cheering, lunging, holding something up.)

A small round-limbed ragdoll made of stitched amber-gold cloth (#e8a72c body,
#c98720 in shadow, warm #f7cf6a highlights, dark brown stitching), with a large
round head nearly half his total height, visible seams along his limbs, and a
soft sheen high on one side of the head. Soft, hand-sewn, a little lopsided — a
toy, not a creature. About a third of the frame tall, feet near the bottom
edge, roughly 82% across.

HE HAS NO EYES AND NO FACE. His only feature is a short row of stitch ticks low
on the head, curving slightly, which reads as a small closed smile. Nothing
else is marked on the head at all — no eyes, no button eyes, no nose, no brows.
Every image model will give him eyes unless the prompt says this twice, which
is why the four scene files repeat it in their NEGATIVE section.

Three rules he breaks constantly if you don't say them: - No eyes. See above;
it needs saying more than once. - He is lit by the scene and pushed toward the
scene's own shadow colour, never glowing at full saturation. A mascot that
stays bright while the world around him goes dark reads as pasted on. - He
casts a soft contact shadow where he meets the ground. Without one he hovers,
however well he's drawn.

Source of truth for the palette is LOOMY_BASE, and for the head geometry the
loomy() function, both in src/renderer/src/lib/loginScenes.ts — the head there
is a plain circle with one highlight and a two-tick stitch run low on it. That
stitch run is the whole face.
